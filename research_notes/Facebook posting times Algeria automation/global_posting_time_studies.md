# Global "best time to post" studies for Facebook Pages and Instagram (2024-2026): times, days, frequency, formats, Meta guidance and reliability

Scope note: research done 2026-11-15. All times below are as stated by each study. Most vendors now normalise to the audience's **local time** (not a fixed zone). Hootsuite also publishes per-zone tables (EST/PST/UTC). Data periods are noted, because several "2026" articles use older data.

## 1. What do the major analytics vendors report as best times/days for Facebook and Instagram (latest studies)? Sample sizes, methodology, time zone

### Takeaway
The big studies **do not agree on hours**. For Facebook: Sprout says weekday midday to evening (Tue/Wed 12-8 pm), Buffer says weekday early morning (8-9 am, Thu 9 am the top slot), and Hootsuite says very early morning (5-8 am, Tue 5 am). They broadly **agree on days**: Monday to Thursday (Wednesday most often) is best, and weekends, especially Saturday or Sunday, are worst. All three express times in the audience's local time. Instagram results also conflict: Later says 3-6 am, Sprout midday to afternoon, Metricool evening (around 8 pm).

### Cited Findings

**Sprout Social: Facebook (2026 data)**
- Best: "Tuesdays and Wednesdays 12-8 p.m. Local Time". Thursday also peaks 12-2 pm and 8 pm, Monday 12-1 pm. Best days Mon-Thu. Weekends are worst, and Sunday is the worst day. — [Sprout Social, Best times to post on Facebook](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Data window Nov 27 2025 to Feb 27 2026. "Nearly 2 billion engagements across 307,000 social profiles" from 30,000+ Sprout customers worldwide, covering 6 networks. The statistical method is not described in detail. The sample is Sprout's own customer base, which skews toward larger brands. — [Sprout Social FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Time zone: each audience's **local time**. — [Sprout Social FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)

**Sprout Social: Instagram (2026 data, same dataset)**
- Mon 2-4 pm, Tue 1-7 pm, Wed 12-9 pm (plus an 11 pm spike), Thu 12-2 pm. Best days Mon-Thu, with Tue/Wed the highest peaks. Weekends are worst. Friday has no significant peak. Avoid 3-7 am. Local time. — [Sprout Social, Best times to post on Instagram](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)

**Buffer: Facebook (page dated Oct 19 2026, data period not stated)**
- More than 14 million Facebook posts sent through Buffer. Uses median engagement (reactions, comments, shares, likes) by hour and weekday. Does not separate out Reels. — [Buffer, Best time to post on Facebook](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Top slot is **Thursday 9 am**. Next are Wednesday 8 am and Sunday 10 am. Best per day: Mon 9 pm, Tue 8 am, Wed 8 am, Thu 9 am, Fri 8 am, Sat 10 pm, Sun 10 am. — [Buffer FB](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Best day is Wednesday, then Thursday, then Tuesday. **Saturday is the worst day.** 12-5 pm afternoons have the lowest engagement on most days. — [Buffer FB](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Time zone: the data was "adjusted" so times apply in your local time zone. The adjustment method is not explained. — [Buffer FB](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)

**Hootsuite: Facebook (article Nov 19 2025)**
- More than 1 million social posts from businesses of all sizes, analysed with the data-science agency Critical Truth. — [Hootsuite, Best time to post on Facebook](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Time zone: "localized across 118 countries where sample data came from". Separate tables are given for EST, PST and UTC. (The MST section is mislabelled "CT", a page error.) — [Hootsuite FB](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Best overall is **Tuesday 5 am**, with activity peaking 5-8 am across the week. Mon 5 pm, Tue 5 am, Wed-Sat 6 am, Sun 8 am. — [Hootsuite FB](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)

**Later: Instagram (article titled 2026, updated Apr 21 2026, but data is Jan-Oct 2024)**
- More than 6M Instagram feed posts (images, videos, carousels, Reels), plus a separate set of more than 975K Reels. Measured by engagement rate over time. Times are normalised to the audience's local time zone. — [Later, Best time to post on Instagram](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)
- Best time **5 am**. Posts made 3-6 am every day beat average engagement. Monday is the best day and Saturday the worst. Worst slots are Thu 3 am and Sat 11 pm. Reels peak at 12 am on Monday. Carousels peak at 5 am on Tuesday. — [Later IG](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)
- Later says to "Take these times with a grain of salt" and to test against your own analytics. The article has internal inconsistencies (for Monday it gives 5 am in one place and 12-6 am in another). — [Later IG](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)
- Inference on the 3-6 am result: content posted before people wake is ready and waiting for the morning scroll. Later does not prove this.

**Metricool (2026)**
- Instagram 2026 study: 24,364,803 posts from 375,118 accounts worldwide. 8 pm is the peak slot on most days, and 6-8 pm is strong all week. (These figures come from the search summary of Metricool's IG page. I did not fetch that page directly.) — [Metricool, Best times to post on Instagram 2026](https://metricool.com/best-time-to-post-instagram/)
- Metricool's cross-network page: Instagram's "06:00 to 09:00 is the sweet spot" and 8 pm has the most views. Best days Wed, then Fri and Tue. Worst is Saturday, then Sun and Thu. Facebook general posts do best 12-2 pm, and the best days are Monday and Wednesday. Facebook Reels peak at "midnight on Saturday". This page gives no sample size, time zone or data year for Facebook. — [Metricool, Best time to post on social networks](https://metricool.com/best-time-to-post-social-networks/)
- Metricool's German Facebook guide says no single hour is best. It notes that Meta stopped providing the relevant audience-online API data on **24 Sep 2024**, so newly connected pages get generic best times instead of page-specific ones. (From the search summary only.) — [Metricool DE, Beste Zeit für Facebook-Posts](https://metricool.com/de/beste-zeit-fuer-facebook-posts/)

**Other / lower quality**
- A 2026 analysis of about 61,000 posts, each scored against the account's own baseline, reports Facebook at 3-5 pm audience local time. This is a small vendor blog, so treat it as weak evidence. — [PostFast data report](https://postfa.st/blog/best-time-to-post-social-media-data-report)

### Inferences
- The only consistent signals across vendors are: (a) Mon-Thu beat weekends, and (b) for Facebook, Saturday or Sunday is the worst day. Hour-level recommendations contradict each other: 5-8 am (Hootsuite), 8-9 am (Buffer), 12-8 pm (Sprout), 12-2 pm (Metricool). Hours should be treated as an untested hypothesis, not a finding.
- Some of the disagreement is about what each study measures. Buffer uses median engagement per post. Sprout uses aggregate engagement volume. Later uses engagement rate. Early-morning winners in engagement-rate studies may reflect less competition rather than more people online.
- **Algeria-specific caution (background knowledge, not sourced in this research):** Algeria runs on UTC+1 all year (no daylight saving time), and its official weekend is **Friday-Saturday**, not Saturday-Sunday. The vendor "weekend is worst" findings come mostly from Western Sat-Sun audiences, so they may not transfer. Sunday is a working day in Algeria. "Local time" in the studies means the poster can schedule in Africa/Algiers time directly, with no conversion needed. Prayer times and Ramadan (when evening and night activity shift) are not modelled by any of these global studies.

### Gaps
- No study reports results for Algeria, MENA or Arabic-language audiences. Hootsuite has 118 countries in its sample but gives no per-country breakdown.
- Buffer's Facebook data period is not stated. Metricool's Facebook sample size and method were not found.
- I did not obtain a dedicated 2025/2026 Facebook timing study from HubSpot, CoSchedule, Socialinsider, Emplifi or Rival IQ within the tool budget.

## 2. Industry-specific results (retail, e-commerce, education, books/publishing, nonprofit/religious)

### Takeaway
Sprout (2026) and Hootsuite (2025) both publish industry splits for Retail, Education and Nonprofit. These categories are the closest proxies for an Islamic/academic bookstore, and they consistently point to **weekday mid-morning to mid-afternoon (roughly 9 am-4 pm local)**, with weekends worst. Neither publishes a books/publishing or religious category.

### Cited Findings
- **Sprout, Facebook, Retail:** Mon 9 am-1 pm. Tue 9 am-2 pm and 5 pm. Wed 9 am-2 pm. Thu 10 am-12 pm. Fri 10 am and 12-1 pm. Sunday is worst. — [Sprout FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)
- **Sprout, Facebook, Education:** Mon 10 am-12 pm. Tue 10 am-12 pm and 2 pm. Wed 10 am-4 pm and 6 pm. Thu 10 am-1 pm and 3-4 pm. Fri 10 am-12 pm. Weekends are worst. — [Sprout FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)
- **Sprout, Facebook, Nonprofit/civic/membership:** Tue 9 am-4 pm. Wed 10 am-6 pm. Thu 10 am-4 pm. Weekends are worst. — [Sprout FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)
- **Hootsuite, Facebook:** Retail/consumer goods 5-6 pm Tue and Thu, and 8-10 am Wed-Sat. Education 1-3 pm Mon, 5 pm Tue, 11 am-1 pm Thu. Nonprofit 10 am-4 pm Mon-Wed. Media and entertainment early morning Thu and Sat. — [Hootsuite FB](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- **Sprout, Instagram:** Education Tue and Wed 11 am, with weekends worst. Retail Mon and Wed 12-3 pm, Tue 12-4 pm, Thu 11 am-5 pm, Fri 12-3 pm. Nonprofit Tue-Thu 11 am-4 pm (the page is slightly inconsistent, also listing Wed 11 am-5 pm). — [Sprout IG](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)
- Emplifi publishes quarterly e-commerce benchmarks (e.g., Q2 2026 worldwide e-commerce) covering format mix rather than timing. — [Emplifi Q2 2026 e-commerce benchmarks](https://emplifi.io/resources/q2-2026-ecommerce-worldwide-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- For a bookstore that is partly retail and partly education, the overlap between Sprout's Retail and Education Facebook windows is about **10 am-1 pm on weekdays**. That is a defensible first slot to test. Hootsuite's Retail evening slot (5-6 pm) is a reasonable second slot.
- "Nonprofit" is the nearest proxy for religious content, but it is a weak proxy. Its window (roughly 10 am-4 pm, Tue-Thu) is similar anyway.

### Gaps
- No vendor study found for books/publishing, bookstores, or religious/Islamic pages.
- Industry splits give no sample size per industry, so their reliability cannot be judged.

## 3. Posting frequency, whether posting too often hurts reach per post, and Reels vs photos vs carousels (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
The common guidance is **1-2 posts/day for Facebook Pages** and **3-5 posts/week for Instagram** feed. Buffer's 2025 Instagram study (2.1M posts) found that reach per post **rose** with frequency, with diminishing returns. It did **not** decline. There is no strong published evidence that one post a day suppresses reach. Sprout warns of "algorithm fatigue" above about 2/day on Facebook, but gives no data for it. On formats, Reels have the highest engagement rate on Facebook in 2026 benchmarks. Photos still make up most brand posts (about 62%), and Reels are growing (about 28%).

### Cited Findings
- **Sprout:** suggests brands post on Facebook "one to two times per day" and warns that more can lead to algorithm fatigue. This is an assertion with no data shown. — [Sprout FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)
- **Buffer:** "You should post on Facebook once a day" (range 1-2/day). This cites a HubSpot study of "over 13,500 Facebook users" (year not given, so likely older data). It applies to Pages, not Groups. — [Buffer, How often to post on social media](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-post-social-media/)
- **Buffer Instagram frequency study (Aug 2025):** 2.1M posts from about 102K accounts, each account compared with itself. Versus 1-2 posts/week, median reach per post was about 12% higher at 3-5/week, about 18% higher at 6-9/week, and about 24% higher at 10+/week. Buffer calls 3-5/week the "sweet spot". — [Buffer, How often to post on Instagram (2025)](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/); figures restated in [Buffer, How often to post on social media](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-post-social-media/)
- Same study, follower growth rate: 1-2/week +0.12%, 3-5/week +0.26%, 6-9/week +0.44%, 10+/week +0.66%. Buffer notes "the lift ... is slightly lower with each jump" (diminishing returns, not decline). It offers no evidence that posting too often reduces reach per post. — [Buffer, How often to post on social media](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-post-social-media/)
- One blog (Apaya) claims Buffer's data shows reach per post *declining* with frequency. This **contradicts Buffer's own write-up**, so Buffer's version is the one to trust. — [Apaya blog](https://apaya.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-social-media) vs [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)
- Buffer has also published a study titled "Consistent Posting Means 5x More Likes, Comments, and Shares". I did not open it, so only the title is known. — [Buffer consistent posting study](https://buffer.com/resources/consistent-posting-study/)
- **Later:** gives no posts-per-week figure. It advises a "predictable posting rhythm" and says consistency matters more than exact minutes. It suggests 2-3/day only to cover audiences in several time zones. — [Later IG](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)
- **Socialinsider (2026 Facebook benchmarks):** brands cut Facebook posting volume by 22% YoY, to about 39 posts/month on average. Reels use grew in 2025 to about 5-7 Reels/month. Reels were the top-engaging format through 2026 (around 0.13-0.15% engagement rate; the quarterly pages disagree on the trend). Albums led on shares and views. Photo engagement reportedly fell from 0.13% to 0.10% in H1 2026, but that comes from a secondary summary. (All from search summaries of Socialinsider's localised pages, not a direct fetch.) — [Socialinsider 2026 Facebook benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/facebook); [Socialinsider Facebook best practices](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/facebook-best-practices/)
- **Emplifi:** photos remain the dominant Facebook post format at about 62% of posts. Reels are rising, to about 28% by Q2 2026. — [Emplifi Q2 2026 e-commerce benchmarks](https://emplifi.io/resources/q2-2026-ecommerce-worldwide-benchmarks/)
- **Meta (Q4 2025):** feed and video ranking improvements gave a 7% lift in views of organic feed and video posts. Facebook is surfacing 25%+ more same-day Reels than in Q3 2025. (From a search summary of Meta's earnings and newsroom materials.) — [Meta newsroom, 2026 AI drives performance](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/2026-ai-drives-performance/amp/)

### Inferences
- One cover image per day sits inside every vendor's Facebook range (1-2/day) and above the Instagram "sweet spot" (3-5/week). Buffer's data suggests 7/week on Instagram is not harmful and may raise per-post reach. There is no evidence that this cadence needs throttling.
- Static single-image posts are the lowest-engagement Facebook format in 2026 benchmarks. Carousels or albums (e.g., front cover, back cover, table of contents) or short Reels made from cover images would probably get more distribution. The Graph API supports multi-photo posts and Reels, but that is outside this research.
- "Same-day Reels" surfacing implies freshness matters for Reels in recommendations. That favours a consistent daily cadence.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2025-2026 study was found on **Facebook Page** frequency vs reach per post. The 1-2/day figure traces back to an undated HubSpot study.
- No primary-source reach comparison of Reels vs photos vs carousels for Facebook Pages. The available benchmarks are engagement-rate based. A Socialinsider "median 365 people reached per post (21 for small pages)" figure appeared only in an unattributed secondary source, so it is excluded as unverified.

## 4. What Meta itself says about timing, and the shift toward recommended/unconnected content

### Takeaway
Meta's official Facebook Feed ranking explainer (updated Oct 6 2026) **does not list post age or timing** as an explicit ranking signal. Ranking is driven by predicted engagement: past interactions, comments, time spent, shares, saves, and content type. Meta has also removed the API metric that told tools when a Page's fans were online (`page_fans_online`, deprecated Sep 2024 and again in the Nov 2025 list). Meanwhile, roughly a third of US Facebook Feed views come from **unconnected/recommended** posts (35.7% in Q1 2025, up from 31.9% in Q3 2024). Followers-at-posting-time therefore matters less than it used to.

### Cited Findings
- Meta's Facebook Feed system card: candidates come from friends, followed Pages and groups. A lightweight model picks about 500 posts, which are then scored by predicted relevance and value. Signals include past interactions with the poster, comment counts, time spent, content type (photo/video/link), and shares and saves. **Post age or recency is not listed as a ranking signal.** "The current time" appears only as an input to predicting "Interested" clicks. Favorites show "their newest posts first", and a "Following" feed exists in reverse chronological order. Page updated Oct 6 2026. — [Meta Transparency Center, Facebook Feed ranking](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-feed/)
- Suggested/recommended content is a separate system: "That content is not powered by the AI we describe in this system card." See the separate Feed Recommendations card. — [Meta, FB Feed ranking](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-feed/); [Meta, Facebook Feed Recommendations AI system](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-feed-recommendations/)
- Widely Viewed Content Report (US): in Q1 2025, **35.7%** of Feed content views came from in-Feed recommendations of "unconnected posts". This was the largest slice that quarter. — [Meta Widely Viewed Content Report](https://transparency.meta.com/data/widely-viewed-content-report) (figure from search summary). In Q3 2024 the figure was 31.9% from unconnected sources. — [Statista, source of Facebook content views US](https://statista.com/statistics/1279138/united-states-source-facebook-news-content-views)
- Meta Q3 2025 earnings call (Oct 29 2025): AI recommendation systems "delivering higher quality and more relevant content" drove +5% time spent on Facebook. — [META Q3 2025 earnings call transcript](https://s21.q4cdn.com/399680738/files/doc_financials/2025/q3/META-Q3-2025-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)
- Instagram: two-thirds of US recommended content came from original posts (Q2 2025), rising to 75% in Q4 2025. This reflects Meta's push for originality. — search summary of Meta materials; [Meta newsroom Jan 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/2026-ai-drives-performance/amp/)
- **Meta's API removed "when fans are online":** the Pages API changelog lists `page_fans_online` among metrics deprecated for all versions on **Sep 16 2024**. It appears again in the Nov 15 2025 deprecation list. The legacy reference doc still describes it as fans online "by hour of day in PST/PDT", but that page is stale. — [Meta Pages API changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/pages-api/changelog); [Meta dev blog, Page Insights API updates (Aug 2025)](https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2025/08/15/page-insights-api-updates/); [Emplifi, Facebook metric deprecation Sep 2024](https://docs.emplifi.io/platform/latest/home/facebook-metric-deprecation-september-2024); [Coupler, metrics deprecation Sep 16 2024](https://help.coupler.io/article/551-facebook-page-insights-metrics-deprecation-on-september-16-2024)
- Metricool confirms the practical effect: since 24 Sep 2024, newly connected pages get generic best times instead of page-specific ones. — [Metricool DE](https://metricool.com/de/beste-zeit-fuer-facebook-posts/)
- Meta Business Suite's Insights and Planner reportedly still show when followers are active, by day and hour, inside the UI. This comes only from third-party blogs (e.g., Shopify, Outfy). I found no official Meta help page confirming it. — [Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook); [Outfy](https://www.outfy.com/blog/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-facebook-in-2025/)
- Instagram (Mosseri, 2025, via secondary sources only): there is "no perfect number" for posting frequency. Watch time, likes and sends are the top signals. Sends weigh slightly more for reaching non-followers. Unconnected distribution tests posts with non-followers. Trial Reels let you test with non-followers first. I could not verify the exact wording from a primary Mosseri post. — [Smash Balloon, IG algorithm 2026](https://smashballoon.com/how-the-instagram-algorithm-works/); [Buffer, Instagram algorithm](https://www.buffer.com/library/instagram-algorithm); [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagrams-posting-tipp-frequenz-wie-lang-pause)

### Inferences
- Timing probably still matters indirectly. A post's early engagement, from followers who are online, feeds the predicted-engagement signals Meta lists. Meta itself, though, frames ranking around relevance, not time slot, and about a third of views now come from recommendations to people who never followed the Page. For a small Page this means content quality, original images and shareability probably matter more than the exact hour.
- The automation cannot pull "fans online by hour" from the Graph API (the metric is gone). To tune timing it would need its own experiment: rotate post times and log per-post reach and engagement from the post-level insights the API still offers.

### Gaps
- I found no official Meta (Meta for Business / Creator / Professional Dashboard) statement saying explicitly whether posting time matters. The Professional Dashboard "best time" feature could not be confirmed from Meta's own docs.
- No Facebook-specific 2025-2026 figure for the share of AI-recommended content beyond the US Widely Viewed Content Report. No figure for Algeria/MENA.

## 5. How reliable are generic "best time" studies vs a page's own audience data? Is there consensus to test with your own Insights?

### Takeaway
Reliability is low at the hour level. The studies contradict each other on hours, are built from each vendor's own customer base (mostly larger, Western brands), use different metrics, and rarely publish full methods. **Every vendor tells readers to use these times as a starting point and to test against their own analytics.** That is the one real consensus.

### Cited Findings
- Hootsuite: "Your ideal posting time ultimately depends on your industry, audience behavior, and time zone". The benchmarks are "a starting point", and "The best way to understand the Facebook algorithm is to track and measure your own performance." — [Hootsuite FB](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)
- Later: "Take these times with a grain of salt". It recommends testing against your own analytics and says consistency beats exact minutes. — [Later IG](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)
- Metricool's own "Best Times" feature is "calculated from specific users' follower behaviors" rather than global averages. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/best-time-to-post-social-networks/)
- Methodology limits: Sprout's sample is its own customers and the method is not detailed ([Sprout FB](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)). Buffer's time-zone "adjustment" is unexplained and its data period is unstated ([Buffer FB](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)). Later's "2026" article uses 2024 data and has internal inconsistencies ([Later IG](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)). Hootsuite's page has a labelling error ([Hootsuite FB](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)).
- Direct contradiction on Facebook hours: Hootsuite's top slot is Tue 5 am, Buffer's is Thu 9 am, and Sprout's is Tue/Wed 12-8 pm. Buffer finds 12-5 pm the *lowest* engagement, while Sprout finds 12 pm onward the *best*. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/); [Sprout](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)

### Inferences
- For an Algerian page with no regional benchmark and no API audience-online data, a practical approach is: start with a weekday-midday slot that most studies support (e.g., about 10 am-1 pm Africa/Algiers, or Sprout-style about 12-2 pm). Rotate among 2-4 candidate slots (e.g., morning around 9 am, midday around 1 pm, evening around 8-9 pm after Isha/Maghrib) over 4-8 weeks. Compare median per-post reach and engagement, and treat the local Fri-Sat weekend separately. Also check Meta Business Suite's audience-activity view manually if it is available.
- Small pages produce noisy per-post data. Compare medians over many posts per slot rather than single posts.

### Gaps
- No independent or academic validation of the vendor studies was found. All the studies are vendor marketing content.
- No published guidance on minimum sample size for a small page's timing tests.
