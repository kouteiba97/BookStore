# Algerian (and Maghreb/MENA) social-media rhythms: when an Algerian bookstore's Facebook/Instagram posts get seen

All times are Algeria local time (UTC+1, no DST) unless stated otherwise. Research date: 2026-11-15.

## 1. Platform reach in Algeria (Facebook vs Instagram vs TikTok), age/gender, and whether Facebook still dominates

### Takeaway
Facebook is still the largest platform in Algeria by ad reach (27.5M, about 57.7% of the population, late 2025). TikTok (24.8M adults 18+) and YouTube (25.4M) are close behind and growing about twice as fast. Instagram reaches about half of Facebook's audience (13.5M) but grows faster than Facebook (+16.9%/yr). The audience skews male on every major platform except Snapchat. Facebook should be the primary channel, with Instagram as a secondary, more gender-balanced audience.

### Cited Findings
- Digital 2026 Algeria (Kepios data, Oct 2025): 37.8M internet users (79.5% penetration), +1.7M (+4.8%) year on year; 9.74M people (20.5%) offline. — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Algeria](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-algeria)
- 27.5M social media user identities (57.7% of the population), +2.6M (+10.2%) year on year. This equals 88.7% of adults 18+ and 72.5% of internet users. Social media identities split 41.6% female and 58.4% male. — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Algeria](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-algeria)
- Platform ad reach, late 2025 (all from the same report). These are platform-reported advertising audiences, not active users:
  - **Facebook: 27.5M (57.7% of population; 78.1% of the eligible 13+ population; 88.7% of adults).** Split 41.6% female / 58.4% male. Up +2.55M (+10.2%) Oct 2024 to Oct 2025.
  - **Instagram: 13.5M (28.4% of population; 38.4% of 13+; 41.6% of adults).** Split 47.5% female / 52.5% male. Up +1.95M (+16.9%) year on year, and +5.1% in the single quarter Jul to Oct 2025.
  - **TikTok: 24.8M adults 18+ (80.1% of adults).** Split 37.1% female / 62.9% male. Up +4.57M (+22.6%) year on year.
  - **YouTube: 25.4M (53.4% of population).** Up +20.4% year on year.
  - **Messenger: 16.2M (34.0%).**
  - **Snapchat: 9.78M**, the only platform with a female majority (52.1%).
  - **LinkedIn: 5.50M members.**
  - **X: 1.07M (2.3%)**, shrinking.
  - — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Algeria](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-algeria)
- DataReportal warns that Meta's audience estimates are "not a proxy for monthly or daily active users, or for engagement." It also advises against recalculating growth from earlier Digital reports, because of corrections in the sources. — [DataReportal, Digital 2026: Algeria](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-algeria)
- A newer DataReportal edition, Digital 2027 Algeria, reports 29.4M social media user identities in Oct 2026 (61.1% of the population). Its platform breakdown was not retrieved in this research. — [DataReportal, Digital 2027: Algeria](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-algeria) (search snippet only)
- NapoleonCat reports about 35.19M Facebook users in Algeria (April 2026). This is from Meta's ad tool and is not comparable with Kepios' de-duplicated figures. — [NapoleonCat, Facebook users in Algeria 2026/04](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/facebook-users-in-algeria/2026/04/) (search snippet only; page not fetched)
- During Bac 2025, Facebook and TikTok were singled out for blocking and described as "the most used [networks] in Algeria." This independently confirms that Facebook is dominant. — [TSA Algérie](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/bac-2025-en-algerie-pas-de-blocage-total-dinternet-une-premiere/)

### Inferences
- Facebook remains the reach channel. About 9 in 10 Algerian adults are reachable there, so the auto-poster should treat Facebook as primary and cross-post to Instagram.
- Instagram's audience is less male-skewed (47.5% female versus 41.6% on Facebook). It is likely younger and more urban, so it suits students and "general readers," especially for visual cover posts and Reels.
- TikTok's fast growth (+22.6%) and male skew (62.9%) make it the main competitor for young users' attention. It is out of scope for a Facebook/Instagram auto-poster, but worth noting.
- Ad-reach figures overstate real daily users. The Page's own Insights should drive the schedule, not these headline numbers.

### Gaps
- No Algeria-specific age-bracket breakdown (13–17, 18–24, 25–34, …) per platform was retrieved. DataReportal's full slide deck usually contains it, but the web text did not.
- The Digital 2027 Algeria platform figures (Oct 2026) were not fetched. Only the headline 29.4M figure was seen in a search snippet.
- No StatCounter Algeria social-media referral share was retrieved.

## 2. Peak social-media hours in Algeria / Maghreb / Arab countries; MENA best-time studies

### Takeaway
No recent, rigorous, Algeria-specific "best time to post" study was found. The available regional evidence is older (2013–2019) and comes from agencies or Meta executives. It consistently points to evening and late-night usage peaks in Arab countries, with activity shifting even later during Ramadan. Generic global best-time studies are computed on US time zones and do not transfer to Algeria. The practical conclusion is to start with an evening-weighted default and let the Page's own Insights decide.

### Cited Findings
- The Online Project's Ramadan study (about 2013, nine Arab countries, Morocco and Algeria not included) found country-specific Facebook peaks:
  - Saudi Arabia: 10 pm
  - Egypt: 4 pm
  - Bahrain: about 11 am
  - Oman: noon
  - Kuwait: 3 pm
  - The study also found social-media usage up about 30% on average during Ramadan, with peak times shifting.
  - — [Wamda, "How the Arab world uses Facebook and Twitter during Ramadan" (2013)](https://wamda.com/2013/07/how-the-arab-world-uses-facebook-and-twitter-during-ramadan) (via search summary; **dated data, and Algeria is not covered**)
- A 2020 report says users in the Middle East spend about 3.5 hours a day on social media. — [IJNet, "Social media in the Middle East: 5 things you need to know"](https://ijnet.org/en/story/social-media-middle-east-5-things-you-need-know) (via search summary)
- French-language scheduling-tool guides (not Algeria-specific) recommend Facebook evening slots of 20:00–23:00 for mobile audiences in "end-of-day scroll" mode. Others suggest 07–09, 13–15 and 19–21. — [Swello](https://swello.com/fr/blog/quand-poster-sur-facebook/); [Codeur.com](https://www.codeur.com/blog/social-media-heures-connexion-audience/); [Metricool FR](https://metricool.com/fr/quand-poster-facebook/) (via search summary)
- Hootsuite's "best times" figures are expressed in US Pacific Time (for example 6:15, 9:15, 12:00–12:30 PT), so they cannot be copied directly to Algeria. — [Hootsuite FR](https://blog.hootsuite.com/fr/meilleurs-moments-pour-publier-sur-facebook/) (via search summary)
- The search did not turn up any study covering peak hours across the Arab world, or any 9–10 pm finding specific to Egypt or Morocco outside Ramadan. — (negative search result; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- A defensible starting default for an Algerian audience is a two-peak workday pattern:
  - a midday or lunch window around 12:00–14:00 (the public-administration lunch break is 12:00–13:00; see §3)
  - a stronger evening window around 19:30–22:30, after work, the evening meal and Maghrib/Isha.
  - Optionally, a light morning post around 08:00–09:00 for commuters and students.
- Weekend (Friday/Saturday) behaviour likely differs. Friday mornings and late evenings are plausible, but Friday midday around Jumu'ah should be avoided (§3).
- Treat these as hypotheses to A/B-test, not established facts for Algeria.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 Algeria- or Maghreb-specific hourly usage dataset was found from Meta, Kepios, NapoleonCat, Sprout, Hootsuite or a local agency (Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia).
- The Arab Social Media Report (Dubai School of Government / MBRSG, last editions around 2017) was not retrieved and is likely too old to matter.
- No Algerian agency blog with Page-Insights-derived peak hours was found in this pass.

## 3. Algerian weekly and daily rhythm: weekend, work hours, school/university, Jumu'ah, Maghrib

### Takeaway
Algeria's weekend has been Friday–Saturday since 14 August 2009, and it is still in place. The working week is Sunday–Thursday. Public administrations work about 08:00–12:00 and 13:00–16:30, with summer timetables in some wilayas from mid-June to end of September. Friday is the main day of rest and prayer, so posting should avoid the Jumu'ah window, roughly 12:30–14:00 local time.

### Cited Findings
- The Friday–Saturday "semi-universal" weekend was generalised nationwide on 14 August 2009. — [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/lalgerie-se-met-au-week-end-semi-universel/); [Afrik.com](https://www.afrik.com/l-algerie-instaure-le-week-end-du-vendredi-et-samedi) (via search summary)
- Neither the administration nor universities operate on Friday. Saturday was made a potentially working day for some institutions, which can adjust their schedules. — [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/la-nouvelle-ere-du-week-end-semi-universel-debute/) (via search summary)
- An executive decree sets public-administration hours at 08:00–12:00 and 13:00–16:30 under normal conditions. The decree's date was not visible in the snippet. — [Algérie360 / Algérie-Eco search results](https://algerie-eco.com/tag/horaire-de-travail/) (via search summary)
- Summer hours: for example, El Meniaa wilaya administrations ran a summer timetable from 14 June to 30 September 2026, Sunday–Thursday, with mornings 07:00–12:00 and reduced afternoons. This shows that summer schedules shift earlier in the south. — [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/administrations-publiques-les-horaires-dete-entrent-en-vigueur-ce-dimanche-14-juin/) (via search summary)
- The private sector has debated or partly adopted a different weekend. There are reports about the private sector and a "universal" weekend, but there is no evidence of a national change; the Friday–Saturday weekend remains official. — [Echorouk](https://www.echoroukonline.com/algerie-le-secteur-prive-adopte-le-week-end-universel/amp) (headline only; not verified)

### Inferences
- **Workdays are Sunday–Thursday; the weekend is Friday–Saturday.** In a cron or scheduler, "weekday" logic must use Sun–Thu, not Mon–Fri.
- **Jumu'ah:** Algiers lies at about 3°E but uses UTC+1, so solar noon is around 12:45–13:00 local time, and Dhuhr/Jumu'ah falls around 12:40–13:05 depending on the season. The khutba, prayer and travel together occupy roughly 12:30–14:00 on Fridays. This is an inference from solar geometry, not from a cited source, so check it against an Algerian prayer-time table (for example the Ministry of Religious Affairs' timetable).
- **Maghrib in Algiers** ranges from about 17:30 in December to about 20:15 in late June (an astronomical inference, not sourced here). Evening family time and dinner follow Maghrib, so the "evening peak" likely moves with the seasons: roughly 20:00–22:00 in winter and 21:30–23:30 in summer.
- School and university days run Sunday–Thursday. Students are likely on their phones in early morning (07:00–08:00), at lunch, and after about 16:30–17:00.
- Saturday is a partial working day for some people and a family or leisure day for others. It is likely a good day for longer-form or "weekend reading" posts.

### Gaps
- No Algerian time-use survey (ONS) on daily screen or social-media time by hour was found.
- No sourced Algerian prayer-time table was retrieved; the Jumu'ah and Maghrib ranges above are inferred.
- No exact university timetable norms were retrieved. Universities typically run about 08:00–17:00 Sun–Thu, but this is not sourced here.

## 4. Ramadan and Islamic calendar: usage shifts and 2027 dates; content opportunities

### Takeaway
During Ramadan, MENA social-media use rises (Meta reported about +5% time spent in 2019; a 2013 agency study found about +30%). Activity shifts later: a peak just before iftar, a strong post-iftar and Taraweeh-to-midnight window, and a late-night or suhoor peak (about midnight to 4 am). Ramadan 2027 is projected to begin about Monday 8 February 2027, with Eid al-Fitr about 9–10 March 2027. Other projected 2027 dates are Eid al-Adha about 16 May 2027, Islamic New Year 1449 about 6 June 2027, and Mawlid about 14–15 August 2027 (all subject to moon sighting).

### Cited Findings
- Facebook's MENA managing director (Ramez Shehadi) said Ramadan brought about 5% more time on Facebook's platforms, about 58 million extra hours (about 2M extra hours a day across the Middle East). He attributed this to people staying up later, more downtime before iftar and suhoor, and shorter working hours. No hourly peaks were given. — [Arab News / AP, "In Mideast, fasting and Facebook go together, data shows" (28 May 2019)](https://www.arabnews.com/media/in-mideast-fasting-and-facebook-go-together-data-shows-1503366)
- In the same piece, Google reported for Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Egypt: +27% time on religious content on YouTube during Ramadan, and +151% for TV dramas and soap operas. — [Arab News (2019)](https://www.arabnews.com/media/in-mideast-fasting-and-facebook-go-together-data-shows-1503366)
- Peak Ramadan social-media hours were reported as just before iftar (about 6–7 pm) and between midnight and 4 am. Sprinklr-linked commentary describes "a substantial increase in online activity around sahoor time and after midnight, as well as after iftar, while people wait for Isha prayers." — [Arab News (Pakistan ed.), "Social media experts weigh in on this year's Ramadan advertising trends"](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2068366/media); [Arab News, "The digital realm's role in Ramadan celebrations…"](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2285301/media) (via search summary; Gulf-centred and secondary, with local times relative to Gulf iftar)
- Users shift towards consuming content rather than creating it during Ramadan. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2285301/media) (via search summary)
- Ramadan raised social-media usage about 30% on average in nine Arab countries, and peak times shifted (about 2013). — [Wamda (2013)](https://wamda.com/2013/07/how-the-arab-world-uses-facebook-and-twitter-during-ramadan)
- **Ramadan 2027:** projected to start on Monday 8 February 2027, with the crescent sought on the evening of Sunday 7 February. Eid al-Fitr is projected for Tuesday 9 or Wednesday 10 March 2027. A few low-quality sites give 5 or 9 February. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/when-will-ramadan-2027-begin-uae-astronomers-share-latest-moon-sighting-forecasts-1.500705567); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/09/02/expected-ramadan-2027-start-date-and-eid-al-fitr-holiday-dates-in-uae/) (via search summary)
- **Eid al-Adha 2027** (10 Dhul-Hijjah 1448): Sunday 16 May or Monday 17 May 2027. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/ramadan-eid-al-fitr-eid-al-adha-2027-likely-dates) (via search summary)
- **Islamic New Year 1449** (1 Muharram): about Sunday 6 June 2027, possibly 7 June. — [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/ramadan-eid-al-fitr-eid-al-adha-2027-likely-dates) (via search summary)
- **Mawlid an-Nabawi** (12 Rabi' al-Awwal 1449): sources differ between Saturday 14 and Sunday 15 August 2027. Ashura (10 Muharram 1449) would be about 15–16 June 2027 (inferred from 1 Muharram, not sourced). — [Dua Travels, Islamic dates 2027](https://duatravels.co.uk/important-islamic-dates-in-2027/); [TrueCalendar Hijri 1449](https://truecalendar.com/hijri/1449) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Ramadan 2027 in Algeria (about 8 Feb – 9 Mar):** Maghrib and iftar in Algiers will fall around 18:10–18:40 (inferred from winter sunset times). A Ramadan schedule might look like this:
  - one post about 17:00–18:00, before iftar, while people are idle and scrolling
  - one post about 21:30–23:30, after Taraweeh (the strongest window)
  - optionally one post about 00:30–02:00, around late-night or suhoor.
  - Mid-morning posts likely underperform, because people sleep later and work hours are shortened.
- Algeria shifts work hours during Ramadan; administrations typically finish earlier. This was not verified for 2027, so treat it as a gap.
- **Content opportunities for an Islamic and academic bookstore:**
  - Ramadan: Qur'an editions, tafsir, hadith, fiqh of fasting, Ramadan reading lists, and gifts.
  - The last ten nights and Laylat al-Qadr (about 3–8 March 2027): spiritual books.
  - Eid al-Fitr: gift books for children, with greetings posts on the day itself and selling posts avoided.
  - Dhul-Hijjah first ten days (about 7–16 May 2027): hajj guides, fiqh of udhiya.
  - Islamic New Year and Hijra: seerah books.
  - Ashura.
  - Mawlid (a public holiday in Algeria, widely celebrated): seerah and shamail, children's seerah.
- On Eid days themselves, family visits likely depress engagement. Greeting posts are fine, but sales pushes should move to Eid evening or the day after (an inference, unverified).

### Gaps
- The user's figure for Ramadan 2026 (about 18 Feb – 19 Mar 2026) was not independently verified in this pass. It is consistent with Ramadan 1447 starting about 18–19 Feb 2026.
- No Algeria- or Maghreb-specific Ramadan hourly data was found. All hourly Ramadan peaks come from Gulf-centred or older sources.
- No 2024–2026 first-party Meta Ramadan data was found. Meta's MENA Ramadan figures retrieved here date from 2019.
- Algeria's official Ramadan 2027 working hours were not retrieved.

## 5. Book-buying seasonality: SILA, rentrée, university year, baccalaureate and internet restrictions

### Takeaway
The key book seasons are:
- SILA, the Algiers International Book Fair, held late October to early November. The 29th edition ran 28 Oct – 7 Nov 2026 at the Palais des Expositions des Pins Maritimes, with Saudi Arabia as guest of honour.
- The school rentrée. Pupils returned on 21 September 2026, after a presidential postponement from 6 September.
- School holidays and exam periods.

Algeria cut the whole country's internet for the full five days of the Bac for years. In 2025 the shutdown became partial: Facebook and TikTok were blocked and there were partial cuts. In 2026 there was no general cut, only localised cuts at and around exam centres. Scheduled posts should still be paused or avoided during Bac exam mornings, because Facebook may be throttled or blocked.

### Cited Findings
- **SILA 2026 (29th edition):** 28 October – 7 November 2026, Palais des Expositions des Pins Maritimes, Algiers. One TSA article gives 29 October as the opening. Saudi Arabia was guest of honour, with more than 200 writers expected and 1,567 publishers from 51 countries (350 Algerian). — [APS (es)](https://www.aps.dz/es/cultura/edicion/mvfh6kac-la-29-%C2%AA-edicion-del-sila-se-celebrara-del-28-de-octubre-al-7-de-noviembre); [TSA Algérie](https://www.tsa-algerie.com/sila-2026-benjamin-stora-yasmina-khadra-john-kaiser-et-plus-de-200-ecrivains-attendus/); [Algérie360](https://www.algerie360.com/sila-2026-salon-international-livre-alger/); [EEAS](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/node/468856_ar) (via search summary)

- **Bac 2024:** connectivity was cut, reportedly only during exam hours. — [Internet Society Pulse, "Internet shutdown for Algeria exams, 10 June 2024"](https://pulse.internetsociety.org/shutdowns/internet-shutdown-for-algeria-exams-10-june-2024) (page returned 404 when fetched; the title and date are from search results only)

### Inferences
- **Key selling windows for the posting calendar:**
  - **Late August to about 21 September:** rentrée. Push school, parascolaire and university titles, and announce new arrivals.
  - **About 1–3 weeks before SILA, through SILA (late October to early November):** strong book interest. Post stand location, SILA promos and "couldn't make it to SILA? order online." The autumn school holidays overlap SILA, so families have time.
  - **Winter holidays (17 Dec – 3 Jan):** reading for children and students.
  - **Ramadan, about 8 Feb – 9 Mar 2027:** the peak for Islamic titles.
  - **Exam periods** (trimester exams, BEM and Bac in May/June): revision books before the exams, then reduced promotional posting during exam days.
  - **Summer:** a lull. The audience is in a holiday mood, and the evening peak shifts later.
- **Bac safeguard for the auto-poster:** keep a configurable "blackout" list. Skip or delay posts on Bac exam days, at least until about 13:00 (morning papers), because Facebook was nationally blocked as recently as June 2025. Even with 2026's localised approach, there is a risk of policy reversal, and student attention is elsewhere. Make the dates configurable, since the Bac 2027 date is unconfirmed (May 9–13 per the ministry calendar, or June per usual practice).
- Islamic events and school dates can collide in 2027. Eid al-Adha (about 16 May) falls just after the 9–13 May exams, and Islamic New Year (about 6 June) falls in the summer exam period. A content calendar should be built from both lists.

### Gaps
- Exact hour-by-hour Bac internet-restriction schedules for 2024 and 2025 were not obtained; the sources give "exam hours" or "morning" only.
- The Bac 2027 official dates are unconfirmed. The ministry listing for terminal-year students says 9–13 May 2027, but TSA does not call it the Bac.
- No data was found on Algerian book-sales seasonality (for example sales volumes by month) or on SILA visitor counts for 2026.
- University calendar 2026–27 dates (rentrée universitaire, typically mid or late September) were not retrieved.

## 6. Using Facebook Page Insights / Meta Business Suite and Instagram Insights to refine the schedule

### Takeaway
Meta Business Suite and the Instagram professional dashboard show a day-by-hour "most active times" view of the account's own followers, based on the past 28 days. Use it to replace the default schedule once the Page has enough followers (a few hundred or more). Note that Instagram shows these times in the device's time zone, so the phone or computer should be set to Algeria time, or the scheduler should convert. Third-party guides describe the feature, but no official Meta Help Center page was retrieved, so menu names should be checked in the live UI.

### Cited Findings
- In Meta Business Suite (desktop), go to Insights → Audience. Instagram's "Most active times" appears as a day/hour heatmap. In the Instagram app, the path is Profile menu → Insights → Total followers → Most active times. — [LeadEnforce, "Meta Business Suite insights"](https://leadenforce.com/blog/meta-business-suite-insights-how-to-analyze-audience-and-content-performance); [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram) (via search summary; third-party)
- The Audience tab shows the days and hours followers were most active over the past 28 days. Older Facebook Page Insights showed this as "When your fans are online" or "When your followers are online." — [Brandwatch](https://www.brandwatch.com/blog/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook) (via search summary; third-party)
- Instagram Insights times are shown "in your device's local timezone, not your audience's timezone." — [Shopify, "Best time to post on Instagram"](https://www.shopify.com/blog/time-post-instagram) (via search summary)
- The feature is available for Business and Creator accounts. ### Inferences
- **Refinement loop for the auto-poster:**
  1. Start with the default (Sun–Thu at about 12:30 and about 20:30–21:30; Friday after about 15:00 and in the evening; Saturday late morning and evening).
  2. After 4 weeks, export per-post reach and engagement, either with the Graph API or manually from Business Suite.
  3. Bucket the results by local hour and weekday.
  4. Compare with the "most active times" heatmap and shift the slots toward the hours with the highest reach per post.
  5. Repeat quarterly and for Ramadan, which needs a separate profile.
- Keep separate schedule profiles for normal periods, Ramadan, summer (later evenings) and the exam or Bac blackout.
- Facebook and Instagram audiences may peak differently. Instagram's is younger and more female, so it may peak later at night. Configure the two platforms independently.
